Six Years After #EndSARS: Nigerians Remember October 20, 2020

Published: 31 October 2026
Category: Nigeria News

Featured Image

October 20, 2020 remains one of the most significant dates in recent Nigerian history. Six years later, the #EndSARS movement continues to represent the demands of many Nigerians for justice, accountability and meaningful police reform.

How the #EndSARS Movement Began

The #EndSARS movement grew from years of complaints about alleged police brutality, harassment, unlawful arrests and abuses attributed to officers of the Special Anti-Robbery Squad (SARS).

Young Nigerians increasingly used social media to share experiences, demand accountability and call for the disbandment of SARS. The movement developed into nationwide demonstrations, with citizens demanding reforms in policing and better protection of human rights.

October 20, 2020: The Lekki Toll Gate Incident

On October 20, 2020, protesters gathered at the Lekki Toll Gate in Lagos during the demonstrations. That evening, security personnel opened fire in an incident that became a defining moment in the history of the movement.

The events, including the number of people injured or killed and the circumstances surrounding the shooting, became subjects of intense public debate, investigations and competing accounts.

Investigations and Calls for Accountability

The Lagos State Judicial Panel of Inquiry investigated the Lekki Toll Gate incident and received testimonies and evidence relating to the events of October 20.

Human rights organisations, including Amnesty International, also called for transparent investigations, accountability and justice for victims of alleged abuses during the protests.

The Nigerian Army disputed some allegations about its conduct and the events of that night. The different accounts have remained an important part of public discussion about the incident.

Six Years Later: What Has Changed?

The anniversary is an opportunity to reflect on the demands that brought thousands of Nigerians into the streets: responsible policing, respect for human rights, justice for victims and stronger accountability institutions.

For many Nigerians, these demands remain important. Remembering October 20 is not only about looking back at the past but also considering what must change to prevent similar incidents in the future.

Why the #EndSARS Movement Still Matters

The movement demonstrated the ability of ordinary citizens, particularly young people, to organise, raise awareness and demand change. It also highlighted the importance of freedom of expression, peaceful protest and the protection of fundamental rights.

Six years later, the message remains clear: citizens deserve safety, dignity, justice and accountable public institutions.
